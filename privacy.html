Privacy policy

Last updated: 17 November 2026

The short version: Nightglide does not collect your data. There is no account, no server run by the developer, no analytics and no advertising. Your drives are stored on your iPhone.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Nightglide app for iPhone and this website. The developer is an individual based in the United Kingdom. Contact: abrahambossey@gmail.com.

What the app stores, and where

All of the following is kept in the app's own storage on your iPhone. None of it is sent to the developer, who has no way to see it.

InformationWhy the app keeps it
Your drives: the route, speeds and times recorded during each driveTo show your trips and work out your score
Events found in each drive (harsh braking, acceleration, cornering, phone handling, speeding)To work out your score and show where events happened
Car details you type in: a name, fuel economy, fuel price, emissions, and MOT, tax and insurance datesTo estimate fuel cost and CO₂, and to remind you of renewal dates
Your settings, and a recording log (when the app woke, checked for a drive, started or saved one; it contains no locations)To run the app and to explain a missed drive

If you back up your iPhone with iCloud or a computer, the app's data is included in that backup like any other app's, under your own Apple account.

Permissions the app asks for

Location, including in the background. Used to notice when a drive starts and to record it, even when the app is closed. Locations stay on your iPhone.

Motion sensors. While a drive is being recorded, the app reads the phone's rotation to notice when the phone is picked up. Readings are not stored; only the resulting event is.

Notifications (optional). Renewal reminders are scheduled by your iPhone itself. Nothing is sent from a server.

When information leaves your iPhone

The app has three features that fetch information over the internet. In each case the request goes straight from your iPhone to the service named, never through the developer.

FeatureWhat is sent, and to whomYour control
Maps on a trip's screenThe map area being shown is requested from Apple Maps.Happens only when you open a trip.
Place names on tripsThe start and end coordinates of each trip are sent to Apple's lookup service to get street names.On by default. Switch off in Settings > Show place names on trips.
Road speed limitsThe map squares a trip passed through (each roughly a mile across) are requested from public OpenStreetMap "Overpass" servers run by volunteer organisations (FOSSGIS e.V. in Germany and OpenStreetMap France). Your route, times and speeds are not sent. Each square is saved on your iPhone so it is asked for only once.Off by default. Switch on in Settings > Check road speed limits.

As with any internet request, these services see your IP address. Apple's handling is described in Apple's privacy policy; OpenStreetMap's in the OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy.

What the app does not do

No account or sign-in. No analytics or crash-reporting kits. No advertising or tracking. No sharing or selling of data, and no link to insurers. The app contains no third-party code.

Exporting and deleting your data

Settings > Export all trips saves a file where you choose. You can delete a single trip from the Trips list, delete every trip in Settings, or remove everything by deleting the app. Because the developer holds none of your data, there is nothing for the developer to delete or hand over.

The "Buy me a coffee" link

The app and this website link to a Buy Me a Coffee page for optional support. That site is run by a separate company with its own privacy policy, and applies only if you choose to visit it.

This website

This site is a static page hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies and has no analytics. GitHub, as the host, logs visitors' IP addresses as described in GitHub's privacy statement.

Children

Nightglide is made for drivers and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. If a future version of the app ever collected data, it would ask first.
